## Slide 2: Introduction

## Slide 3: Problem Statement
- Define the core ethical dilemma: Who is accountable when autonomous vehicle software causes harm?
- Explain the complexity of responsibility in multi-stakeholder systems.
- Mention examples like safety vs. speed of innovation and incomplete testing.
- Identify key stakeholders: software engineers, corporations, regulators, drivers, and the public.

## Slide 4: Objectives
- To understand ethical challenges in developing autonomous systems.
- To evaluate real-world case studies involving AV companies.
- To propose practical recommendations for ethical software and system design in AVs.

## Slide 6: Methodology
- Describe the approach: comparative case study of four AV companies (Apple, Tesla, Waymo, BYD).
- Explain use of ethical frameworks: 
  - Utilitarianism (maximizing public good),
  - Duty Ethics (engineers’ obligations),
  - Stakeholder Analysis (who is impacted and how).
- Justify case selection: public visibility, technological relevance, and diversity of approach.

## Slide 7: Case Study – Apple (Project Titan)
- Discuss Apple’s secretive AV project and its discontinuation after a $10B investment.
- Highlight ethical concerns: lack of transparency, frequent pivots, internal confusion.
- Analyze whether the project was ethically managed despite being a business failure.

---

## Slide 8: Case Study – Tesla (Autopilot)
- Explore ethical issues around Tesla’s Autopilot, a Level 2 system often misunderstood as fully autonomous.
- Mention accidents caused by user overtrust and lack of monitoring.
- Evaluate ethical implications of marketing, design, and responsibility.

---

## Slide 9: Case Study – Waymo (Google)
- Present Waymo’s transparent, safety-driven approach to Level 4 autonomous driving.
- Discuss how they publish safety data and collaborate with regulators.
- Highlight alignment with ethical codes and proactive responsibility.

---

## Slide 10: Case Study – BYD (China)
- Introduce BYD as a company that prioritizes safety and gradual innovation.
- Contrast their conservative AV strategy with more aggressive rollouts.
- Analyze how caution and testing can reflect strong ethical engineering practices.

---

## Slide 11: Ethical Issues and Analysis
- Identify major ethical concerns in AV development:
  - Safety
  - Transparency
  - Responsibility and blame
- Discuss the “Moral Crumple Zone” — where engineers may be unfairly blamed in complex systems.
- Evaluate the conflict between innovation speed and public welfare.

---

## Slide 12: Solutions and Recommendations
- **Technical**: Implement robust simulation, edge-case testing, real-time monitoring.
- **Ethical**: Promote internal ethical review boards, ethics training, and whistleblower support.
- **Policy**: Suggest stronger government oversight, safety audit trails, and mandatory transparency reports.

---

## Slide 13: Role of Engineers
- Emphasize engineers' responsibility to protect public welfare through ethical design.

- Discuss how engineers can navigate corporate pressure while upholding safety and transparency.
